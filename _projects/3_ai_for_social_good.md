As AI grows, so does the computing infrastructure behind it, and data centers have drawn strong pushback from communities across the political spectrum. In 2025, the University of Michigan and Los Alamos National Laboratory announced a high-performance research computing center in Ypsilanti, Michigan. As part of an **AI for Social Good Carpentry** team, we asked how experts and community members in Washtenaw County see such centers, how far they trust the information available, and how a communication tool could help the two groups talk to each other.

We ran in-depth interviews (about 60 minutes each) with 10 participants, 5 experts and 5 community members, combined with a trust survey and speculative design. We found a clear information gap and mistrust between data scientists and community members, and experts raised concerns about their personal safety and about misinformation on data science. Both groups asked for transparent impact reports with continuous environmental data, more respect for everyone involved, and an online tool for project updates alongside in-person outreach from developers.

- **CCST & California Natural Resources Agency Science Day 2026** (poster, Oct 2026): _Bridging the AI Infrastructure Divide: Navigating Public Backlash, Researcher Safety, and the Polarization of Data Centers_.
