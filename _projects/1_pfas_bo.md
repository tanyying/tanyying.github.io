**Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS)** are called "forever chemicals" because their carbon–fluorine bonds are among the strongest in chemistry, so they persist in water, soil and our bodies. Adsorption is one of the most practical ways to remove them, but finding a good, _sustainable_ adsorbent means searching a large space of compositions and synthesis conditions, and each experiment takes time.

I combine corncob, an abundant agricultural residue, with PVDF to make composite chars and hydrochars. Rather than testing recipes one by one, I use **Bayesian optimization** to decide which experiment to run next. It balances exploring the unknown with exploiting what already works, so it reaches high-performing materials in far fewer experiments.

This work is part of my **Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellowship** at the [Michigan Institute for Data & AI in Society (MIDAS)](https://midas.umich.edu/), mentored by Prof. Anne J. McNeil and Prof. Bryan R. Goldsmith. Prof. Paul M. Zimmerman serves on my committee.
