Textile and palm-oil industries release large volumes of colored, hard-to-treat wastewater. Meanwhile, agriculture and everyday life produce tonnes of biomass "waste". My Ph.D. at **Universiti Malaya** brought the two together through a **circular-economy** approach: convert low-value waste into high-value **activated carbons and composites** that adsorb dyes and other recalcitrant pollutants.

- **Novel precursors:** passion fruit (_Passiflora edulis_) leaves, palm kernel shells, lignocellulosic crop residues, and waste human hair.
- **Engineered surfaces:** magnetic graphene oxide and iron-oxide impregnation for higher capacity and easy magnetic recovery.
- **Process optimization:** response-surface methods (e.g., Box–Behnken design), kinetics, isotherms, and mechanism studies.
- **Practicality:** cost analysis showing these materials can compete with commercial adsorbents.
- **Toward AI:** machine-learning models of dye adsorption, which earned a Best Poster Award at Peking University in 2024.

I also carried out research abroad, at **Polytechnique Montréal** (2022) with Prof. Gregory S. Patience and at **LRGP, Université de Lorraine** (2023–2024) with Prof. Marie-Noëlle Pons, supported by the Embassy of France to Malaysia.
