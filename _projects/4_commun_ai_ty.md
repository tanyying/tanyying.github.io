As AI shows up in more and more of science, researchers need to explain _what the model did_, _why it can be trusted_, and _where it can't_. They need to say this clearly to other scientists, to policymakers, and to the public.

I am the lead organizer of **Commun-AI-ty: Building Science Communication Skills for Explaining AI in Science**, a workshop funded by the **Schmidt Sciences Initiative Fund** ($93,970).

Related work on AI, science and society:

- **Poster**, CCST & California Natural Resources Agency Science Day 2026: _Bridging the AI Infrastructure Divide: Navigating Public Backlash, Researcher Safety, and the Polarization of Data Centers_.
- **Judge**, AI Institutes at Michigan (AIIM) Empowering Research with AI Award (2026); Data Science and AI Summit poster session (2025); U-M Chemical Engineering Graduate Symposium (2025).
