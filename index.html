Yan Ying Tan

Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow at MIDAS, University of Michigan.

Michigan Institute for Data & AI in Society

University of Michigan

Ann Arbor, MI, USA

I’m a chemical engineer who works where sustainable materials meet machine learning. As a Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow at the Michigan Institute for Data & AI in Society (MIDAS), University of Michigan, I work with Prof. Anne J. McNeil and Prof. Bryan R. Goldsmith on using Bayesian optimization to design polymer- and biomass-derived chars that capture PFAS (“forever chemicals”) from water, so we find high-performing materials with far fewer costly experiments.

I earned my Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering (Advanced Materials & Technology) from Universiti Malaya, Malaysia. There I turned agricultural and everyday waste, such as passion fruit leaves, palm kernel shells and even human hair, into activated carbons that remove dyes and other stubborn pollutants from wastewater. Along the way, I did research stints at Polytechnique Montréal (Canada) and the Université de Lorraine (LRGP, France).

Outside the lab, I care about making science understandable. I’m the lead organizer of Commun-AI-ty, a workshop funded by Schmidt Sciences that builds science-communication skills for explaining AI in science.

Research interests: circular economy, machine learning, Bayesian optimization, green materials, sustainability, AI for science.
